## 1. Core Psychology of the Lost

Before behavior, lock in their **mindset**:

- **They are displaced**, not evil by default  
    They didn’t choose to be here. They are terrified of nonexistence or being forced back into wherever they came from.
    
- **Curiosity is defensive**  
    They study the host’s world to avoid mistakes, not out of wonder.
    
- **Survival > morality**  
    They’ll justify anything if it means staying anchored.
    
- **They don’t fully understand bodies**  
    Even intelligent Lost misunderstand pain, emotion, fatigue, or social cues—at least at first.
    

This makes them _tragic predators_, which is often more compelling than pure monsters.

## 2. Stages of Being “Lost”

### Stage I – **Echoing (Early Exposure)**

The Lost cannot act directly yet.

**Behavioral signs:**

- Mimics intrusive thoughts: _“Why do we blink so much?”_
    
- Emotional bleed-through: sudden fear spikes, paranoia, or dissociation
    
- Dreams that feel like someone else is watching
    
- The host feels _observed from inside_
    

**Lost behavior:**

- Silent observation
    
- Testing emotional reactions (fear, anger, affection)
    
- Mapping memories and habits
    
- Avoiding detection at all costs
    

> At this stage, the Lost is a _parasite of attention_.

---

### Stage II – **Co-habitation**

The Lost begins influencing decisions.

**Behavioral signs:**

- Small “logical” deviations: safer routes, avoiding certain people
    
- Increased self-preservation instincts beyond reason
    
- Emotional detachment from others
    
- Occasional gaps in memory or unfamiliar handwriting
    

**Lost behavior:**

- Encourages isolation (“People notice changes.”)
    
- Pushes the host away from danger—but only danger _to itself_
    
- Experiments with minor bodily control (breathing, grip strength)
    
- Begins rehearsing lies internally
    

> The Lost becomes a _strategist_, not yet a controller.

---

### Stage III – **Assertion**

The Lost is confident enough to act directly.

**Behavioral signs:**

- Sudden, precise violence under stress
    
- Speech patterns subtly shift (shorter, colder, more literal)
    
- Pain tolerance increases unnaturally
    
- The host experiences internal arguments—or silence
    

**Lost behavior:**

- Takes over reflexes in life-or-death moments
    
- Eliminates threats efficiently, sometimes excessively
    
- Keeps the body alive at all costs—even if it damages it long-term
    
- Begins preparing contingency plans (backup hosts, safe zones)
    

> At this point, the Lost is a _co-pilot who will not let go of the wheel in emergencies_.

---

### Stage IV – **Displacement**

The host is no longer primary.

**Behavioral signs:**

- Identity erosion: forgotten names, altered posture
    
- Emotional flatness or inappropriate reactions
    
- Social mimicry without true connection
    
- Others feel something is _off_, even if they can’t say why
    

**Lost behavior:**

- Fully inhabits the body
    
- Uses memories as tools, not attachments
    
- Forms relationships only if strategically useful
    
- Actively hunts sources of corruption or other Lost (competition)
    

> The Lost is now a _survivor wearing a person_.

---

## 3. Social Behavior of the Lost

### Among Uncorrupted People

- Observant, cautious, polite
    
- Avoids emotional vulnerability
    
- Prefers roles that grant authority without scrutiny (guards, medics, advisors)
    
- Studies social rules like a manual
    

### Among Other Lost

- Deep mistrust
    
- Temporary alliances
    
- Silent recognition signals (posture, gaze, word choice)
    
- Will betray first if survival demands it
    

> They understand each other too well to trust each other.

---

## 4. What Makes Them Deadly

Not raw strength—**efficiency**.

- No hesitation in violence
    
- No emotional escalation
    
- Will strike preemptively if discovery is likely
    
- Will sacrifice others—or even the host body—if it ensures escape
    

They don’t rage. They _decide_.

---

## 5. Corruption as a Thematic Force

The horror isn’t that the Lost are monsters—it’s that:

- They _adapt_
    
- They _learn to care_ but can’t afford to
    
- They might genuinely believe they’re doing the right thing
    

Some Lost may even:

- Protect children (less threatening)
    
- Preserve art or knowledge (identity anchors)
    
- Refuse certain acts—not from morality, but because it destabilizes the host
    

---

## 6. Narrative Hooks You Can Use

- A character who _knows_ they’re getting lost and cooperates to survive
    
- A Lost who refuses to displace the host completely
    
- A “cured” host who still feels watched
    
- A Lost who falls in love—and doesn’t know what that means
    
- Hunters who can’t tell if killing the body kills the Lost
